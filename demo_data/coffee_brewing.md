# Coffee Brewing Basics

## Grind size

Grind size controls extraction speed. Espresso needs a fine, powdery grind because water is forced through in about 25–30 seconds. French press uses a coarse grind so grounds do not pass through the metal filter and the coffee does not over-extract during a four-minute steep. Pour-over sits in the middle with a medium grind, similar to table salt.

## Water temperature

The recommended brewing temperature is 92–96 °C (195–205 °F). Water that is too hot extracts bitter compounds; water that is too cool leaves the coffee sour and weak. Letting a boiling kettle rest for 30 seconds usually lands in the right range.

## Ratio

A common starting ratio is 1:16 — one gram of coffee to sixteen grams of water. For a single mug that is roughly 15 g of coffee to 240 g of water. Espresso uses a much tighter ratio, around 1:2.

## Freshness

Coffee is best 4–21 days after roasting. Store beans in an airtight container away from light and do not refrigerate them; condensation ruins the flavour.
